# Why AU4 and AU7? (Provisional Baseline)

- **AU4 — brow lowerer:** A visible brow-lowering movement potentially associated with frowning, concern, or concentration.
- **AU7 — lid tightener:** A tightening around the eyelids that can accompany tension or concentration.

We chose these as **simple, observable candidate facial cues**, not reliable stand-alone indicators of sadness, stress, or emotional distress. Their meaning depends on context. Our baseline therefore requires **a facial cue AND a verbal distress cue** before issuing a check-in. In `--binary` mode a human marks AU4-*like*/AU7-*like* movements; this is **not automatic or formally validated FACS coding**. The older numeric threshold of 45 is provisional and is not used by binary mode.

**Research alignment:** Our P1 proposal cites **Gentsch et al. (2015)** on relationships between facial muscle activity and emotional appraisal, while cautioning against treating any single movement as proof of emotion. It also cites **Zimmermann et al. (2011)** (VR-CoDES) for verbal expressions of concern. Those sources support combining facial behavior and verbal context; **they do not establish AU4/AU7 as validated distress markers or determine our threshold**.

**Next step:** When eEVA returns, compare these manually observed cues against actual AU outputs and revise the cue selection based on data and feedback.
